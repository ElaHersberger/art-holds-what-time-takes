## Overview

The Wall Art Consultation is a developing, person-first approach to selecting fine art for interiors.

It begins with understanding the individual rather than immediately selecting artwork based on furniture, colors, or wall measurements.

The central belief is simple:

**A room should tell the story of the person who lives there.**

Art should reflect that person's character, bring harmony to the interior, and contribute to the emotional identity of the home.

The methodology consists of five proposed phases.

## Phase 1 — Personal Discovery

**Purpose:** Understand the person before considering the artwork.

Two foundational discovery questions:

**Question 1:**

"If you had an entire day off, unlimited money, and no obligations, what would you do?"

This question explores the client's aspirations, interests, and sources of happiness.

**Question 2:**

"Show me three things you own that you love the look or feel of."

The objects may include furniture, clothing, jewelry, textiles, antiques, or treasured possessions.

Explore what attracts the client to each object.

Possible qualities include texture, color, craftsmanship, nostalgia, elegance, simplicity, or emotional meaning.

**Desired outcome:** A preliminary understanding of the client's character and aesthetic preferences.

## Phase 2 — Character Interpretation

**Purpose:** Identify the artistic qualities suggested by the client's preferences.

Interpret the client's responses through descriptive concepts such as:

- Sophistication
- Elegance
- Warmth
- Adventure
- Tranquility
- Individuality
- Nostalgia
- Connection to nature

These are interpretations to discuss and validate with the client, not fixed personality classifications.

**Desired outcome:** A shared understanding of the client's aesthetic identity.

## Phase 3 — Artistic Translation

**Purpose:** Translate personal preferences into artistic direction.

Consider:

- Color palette
- Composition
- Texture
- Artistic style
- Materials and finishes
- Emotional atmosphere

### Example

A client selects three meaningful objects:

A soft cashmere sweater, an antique wooden table, and a crystal vase.

Ela interprets these choices as suggesting luxury, sophistication, and elegance.

Her proposed artistic direction includes natural colors, sap green or deep blue, and delicate gold details.

This example demonstrates how personal preferences may inform artistic recommendations.

It is an illustrative exercise, not a documented completed client project.

**Desired outcome:** An initial artistic direction grounded in the individual's preferences.

## Phase 4 — Spatial Harmony

**Purpose:** Establish a relationship between the artwork and its environment.

Evaluate:

- Architecture
- Wall dimensions
- Furniture placement
- Color relationships
- Natural and artificial lighting
- Artwork proportions
- Visual balance
- Materials and textures

### Guiding Principle

**Harmony does not require uniformity.**

A room does not need to be designed in one color or one style.

Deep blue artwork can create a beautiful relationship with cream furnishings. Gold details can connect warm woods with contemporary artistic elements.

Eclectic pieces can belong together when their relationships are intentional.

Artwork may become the starting point for the room or the finishing element in an existing interior.

**Desired outcome:** An artistic direction that connects personal taste with the physical space.

## Phase 5 — Artwork Selection

**Purpose:** Help the client select fine art they will enjoy living with.

Potential considerations include:

- Emotional connection
- Artistic quality
- Appropriate scale
- Color harmony
- Original artwork versus limited editions
- Materials and presentation
- Placement and installation requirements
- Budget and available payment options

The goal is not simply to fill an empty wall.

It is to identify artwork that feels meaningful to the client and belongs naturally within the interior.

**Desired outcome:** A considered artwork recommendation that reflects the person and the space.

## Client Objections

### Price

**Documented client language:**

"I love it, but it's more than I expected."

Ela's preferred approach includes exploring available payment options rather than immediately discounting artwork.

The consultation should respect the client's financial preferences and explain artwork options without pressure.

### Artwork Size

Clients may struggle to visualize how different artwork dimensions will appear in their homes.

Potential solutions include wall measurements, interior photographs, size comparisons, and digital placement mockups.

These tools are proposed components of the developing service.

## Foundational Principles

**Personality Before Palette**

Understand the person before selecting artistic direction.

**Harmony Without Uniformity**

Contrast and eclectic elements can create visual connection.

**Connection Before Decoration**

Artwork should contribute to the identity of the space.

**Art as Personal Storytelling**

A room should reflect the individual who lives there.

**Art Remains**

Fine art should offer lasting enjoyment and meaning.

"ART REMAINS." is the established brand signature of Masterpiece Artwork.

## Relationship to My Process

The Wall Art Consultation is distinct from Ela Hersberger's original artistic framework:

**Surrender → See → Honor → Reveal → Hold**

My Process describes her overarching artistic philosophy.

The Wall Art Consultation is a developing practical application focused on fine art selection and interiors.

The two frameworks are complementary but should not be represented as identical.
