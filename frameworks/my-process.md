# My Process

## Overview
My process begins in surrender, not control. It is a sequential practice for recognizing love and giving it permanent artistic form. It operates on a simple conviction: surrender makes room to see. Seeing allows you to honor. Honor reveals truth. Art gives that truth permanence.

## The Five Steps
1. **Surrender:** Release ego and control. Begin with faith, prayer, and listening. The artist becomes a vessel, not a manufacturer.
2. **See:** Look beyond performance and posing. Discover the emotional truth, relationships, and gestures already present.
3. **Honor:** Treat every subject with dignity and extraordinary care. Create safety so people can be themselves without feeling extracted.
4. **Reveal:** Use technical mastery in service of truth. Translate recognition through light, composition, styling, and classical form.
5. **Hold:** Give the revealed truth a physical, heirloom form. Accept that life changes, but love deserves a permanent place to live.

## Why It Matters
Disposable culture treats memory as clutter. My Process treats it as lineage. It filters out the transactional and attracts the family who understands that preservation requires reverence, patience, and care.

## What It Replaces
The rush to capture without context. The belief that high-resolution equipment equals high-resolution meaning. The assumption that digital folders preserve feeling.

*Art holds what time takes.*

# The Collector

## Overview
The Collector defines the specific human experience Art Holds What Time Takes is built for. This is not a demographic segment. It is a psychological and behavioral alignment with permanence, craftsmanship, and lineage.

## Who They Are
Discerning families and couples—from parents raising children to grandparents preserving a legacy. They value family identity, emotional connection, and classical beauty. They are defined by values, not wealth alone. They prefer fewer, deeply meaningful pieces over hundreds of digital files.

## The Invisible Problem
They feel time moving too quickly. Love is felt but invisible. Digital clutter replaces physical presence. Children grow up without daily evidence of their place in the family.

## What They Actually Want
- **For today:** Their children see themselves as loved, valued, and securely part of the family.
- **For tomorrow:** An enduring work of art that preserves this season after it has passed.

## The Shift
From: Hoping digital folders preserve feeling, treating imagery as a quick transaction, evaluating technical execution.
To: Commissioning structural permanence, treating artwork as emotional care, witnessing recognition and belonging.

*Collectors do not purchase images. They secure lineage.*
