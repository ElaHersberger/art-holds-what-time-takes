# My Process
## The Artistic Philosophy of Ela Hersberger

**Founder:** Ela Hersberger  
**Framework:** Surrender · See · Honor · Reveal · Hold  
**Status:** Established Artistic Framework  
**Application:** Fine Art, Personal Storytelling, Commissioned Artwork, and Artistic Interpretation

---

## Overview

My process begins in surrender, not control.

I believe meaningful art begins with understanding the person, their story, their character, and the emotions that make them who they are.

Art is not simply about creating something beautiful.

It is about recognizing what matters and giving it artistic expression.

My process follows five principles:

**Surrender. See. Honor. Reveal. Hold.**

Originally developed through my work creating meaningful family artwork, these principles continue to guide my artistic philosophy as I expand into fine art curation and wall art design.

## The Architecture

### 1. Surrender — Begin With Openness

Release ego, assumptions, and the need to impose a predetermined artistic vision.

Listen before creating.

Understand the person, their experiences, dreams, and relationship with beauty.

In family artwork, this means allowing genuine relationships and emotions to emerge.

In fine art curation, it means setting aside personal preferences long enough to discover what genuinely resonates with the client.

**What the client receives:** Trust and understanding.

### 2. See — Recognize What Matters

Look beyond appearance, status, trends, and surface-level preferences.

Notice the details that reveal character.

The objects someone treasures. The colors they gravitate toward. The textures they enjoy. The experiences that bring them joy.

Recognize the relationship between the individual and their surroundings.

**What the client receives:** Recognition and personal connection.

### 3. Honor — Respect Individuality

Every person deserves to feel understood and respected.

Do not force someone into a predetermined artistic style or design trend.

Honor their history, personal taste, emotional connections, and individuality.

In commissioned family artwork, this means creating an experience rooted in dignity, patience, and care.

In art for interiors, it means helping the client discover artwork that feels authentic to who they are.

**What the client receives:** Belonging and confidence.

### 4. Reveal — Translate Identity Into Art

Use artistic knowledge, sensitivity, and craftsmanship to express what has been discovered.

Through color, composition, light, texture, scale, and visual relationships, bring personal meaning into artistic form.

In family commissions, this may involve storytelling, gesture, styling, and classical artistic techniques.

In wall art design, it may involve selecting artwork that creates harmony between the individual, the architecture, and the interior.

Harmony does not require everything to match.

Different colors, materials, styles, and artistic influences can come together to create something deeply personal.

**What the client receives:** Artistic expression of their individuality.

### 5. Hold — Give Meaning a Place to Live

Art should not exist only as an image or temporary experience.

It deserves a meaningful place in our lives.

A work of art can become part of a family's history, a home's identity, and a person's everyday experience.

Its presence can bring beauty, emotion, connection, and joy.

The goal is not simply to fill a wall.

It is to create something meaningful that belongs within the space and the life of the person who lives there.

**What the client receives:** A lasting relationship with art.

---

## Why It Matters

Art should reflect the person, not simply follow a trend.

A beautiful room does not require perfectly matched objects.

It requires connection.

Connection between the person, the artwork, the architecture, and the surrounding environment.

The same philosophy that guides meaningful family artwork can also inform the selection of fine art for interiors.

**The person comes first.**

---

## What It Replaces

This approach challenges:

- Artwork selected solely to match furniture
- Generic decoration without personal meaning
- Trend-driven artistic decisions
- The belief that beautiful interiors require uniformity
- Transactional art selection without understanding the individual
- Artwork treated as an afterthought

Within family portraiture, it also challenges performance-based posing, disposable imagery, and the belief that meaningful art requires manufactured perfection.

## First-Person Explanation

"My process begins with understanding the person.

Before I think about color, composition, or where the artwork will live, I want to know who they are.

What brings them joy? What do they love? What makes their story unique?

I listen. I observe. I honor their individuality.

Then I use my artistic vision to bring those discoveries into something meaningful.

I believe art should create harmony, not simply match the furniture.

A room should tell the story of the person who lives there.

And the artwork should become part of that story."

— Ela Hersberger

## Relationship to the Wall Art Consultation

**My Process** is the overarching artistic philosophy.

**The Wall Art Consultation** is a separate, emerging practical methodology for applying that philosophy to artwork selection and interiors.

The proposed consultation phases are:

1. Personal Discovery
2. Character Interpretation
3. Artistic Translation
4. Spatial Harmony
5. Artwork Selection

These frameworks are related but serve different purposes.

My Process describes the principles behind Ela's artistic decisions.

The Wall Art Consultation describes how those principles may be applied in a structured client experience.

The consultation framework remains in development.
